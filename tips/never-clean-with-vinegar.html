7 Things You Should Never Clean with Vinegar

Vinegar is one of the best natural cleaners out there — cheap, effective and safe on most surfaces. But its acid is exactly what makes it risky on a few specific materials. Here are the seven things to keep vinegar away from, and what to use instead.

What you need

  • A pH-neutral stone cleaner (for marble and granite)
  • Wood floor cleaner made for your floor type
  • Hot water and mild dish soap (the safe default for most of these)
  • Microfiber cloths

The seven things

  1. Natural stone — marble, granite, travertine: vinegar's acid etches and dulls stone, leaving permanent marks. Use a pH-neutral stone cleaner instead.
  2. Hardwood floors: vinegar strips the finish over time and dulls the wood. Use a cleaner made for hardwood floors.
  3. Cast iron skillets: vinegar strips the seasoning you worked so hard to build. Clean with hot water, dry immediately, and re-oil lightly.
  4. Aluminum and copper pots: vinegar can pit aluminum and discolor copper. Use warm soapy water instead.
  5. Waxed furniture and floors: vinegar dissolves wax finishes. Use a damp cloth with a little dish soap.
  6. Electronic screens: vinegar can damage anti-glare coatings on TVs, phones and laptop screens. Use a dry or slightly damp microfiber cloth.
  7. Rubber seals and gaskets: repeated vinegar use can dry out and crack rubber seals on dishwashers and washing machines. Wipe them with warm soapy water instead.
Good to know: When in doubt, the safe default is warm water with a drop of dish soap — it cleans almost everything on this list without damaging anything.
Safety first: Never mix vinegar with bleach — it creates chlorine gas, which is toxic. Never mix vinegar with hydrogen peroxide in the same bottle either — it makes peracetic acid, which irritates skin, eyes and lungs. Use one cleaner at a time and rinse between products.
