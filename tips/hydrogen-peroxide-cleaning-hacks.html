Hydrogen Peroxide Cleaning Hacks

The brown bottle in the first-aid kit is also a great household cleaner. Standard 3% hydrogen peroxide disinfects, whitens grout and removes stains — as long as you use it correctly and never mix it with the wrong things.

What you need

  • 3% hydrogen peroxide (the standard drugstore bottle)
  • A dark spray bottle (light breaks peroxide down)
  • An old toothbrush
  • Microfiber cloths
  • Rubber gloves

How to do it

  1. Whiten grout: spray peroxide on grout lines, wait 10 minutes, scrub with a toothbrush and rinse.
  2. Sanitize cutting boards: after washing with soap, spray the board with peroxide, wait 5 minutes, rinse well and air-dry.
  3. Clean the toothbrush holder: soak it in peroxide for 10 minutes, rinse and dry.
  4. Brighten white laundry: add 1 cup of 3% peroxide to the wash with white loads (not colors) instead of bleach.
  5. Deodorize the trash can: spray the inside, wait 10 minutes, wipe and let it dry in the sun if possible.
  6. Clean the toilet bowl: pour 1/2 cup into the bowl, wait 20 minutes, scrub and flush.
  7. Shine mirrors and glass: spray lightly and wipe with a dry microfiber cloth for a streak-free finish.
Good to know: Keep peroxide in its original dark bottle and store it in a cool, dark place — sunlight and heat make it break down into plain water, so it stops working.
Safety first: Use only 3% household hydrogen peroxide — never stronger concentrations. Never mix hydrogen peroxide with vinegar (it makes peracetic acid, which irritates skin, eyes and lungs) and never mix it with bleach. Use one product at a time, rinse between products, and test on a hidden spot first — peroxide can lighten colored fabrics and some surfaces.

What to buy

  • 3% hydrogen peroxide, multi-pack
  • Dark amber spray bottles
  • Grout brush set
