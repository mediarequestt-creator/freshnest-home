Fridge Organization Ideas That Actually Stick

Most fridge systems fail because they are too complicated to keep up. This one is simple: every shelf has a job, everything is visible, and a five-minute reset once a week keeps it working. Less wasted food, less time hunting.

What you need

  • Clear stackable bins (2–4, sized to your shelves)
  • A lazy Susan turntable for condiments
  • Labels or a marker
  • Baking soda for odors
  • A trash bag for expired items

How to do it

  1. Empty and wipe: take everything out, toss expired items, and wipe the shelves with warm soapy water.
  2. Set the zones: top shelf for leftovers and ready-to-eat food, middle shelf for dairy and eggs, bottom shelf for raw meat (in a tray), drawers for produce, door for condiments.
  3. Bin by category: one bin for snacks, one for sauces, one for breakfast items. If you can see it, you will use it.
  4. Turntable for condiments: small jars get lost in the door — a lazy Susan spins them into view.
  5. First in, first out: put new items behind older ones so the older food gets used first.
  6. Label leftovers: masking tape and a date on every container — no more mystery boxes.
  7. Weekly 5-minute reset: before grocery day, toss anything expired, wipe one shelf, and put the open box of baking soda back on the shelf.
Good to know: Keep the fridge between 35–38°F (2–3°C). Food stored above 40°F (4°C) spoils much faster, no matter how tidy the shelves look.

What to buy

  • Clear fridge bins, set of 4
  • Lazy Susan turntable
  • Baking soda, multi-pack
